**On the Creativity Question (of Artificial Intelligence)**

Lately, I’ve had a number of different thoughts around the question of artificial intelligence and creativity. Here, I am ~~penning~~ typing them down, hoping to find some coherence. Big disclaimer: I have no background in cognitive science, this is just me wondering out loud\!

An attempt at defining creativity is probably a good starting point. I consider something to be creative when it is novel and profound. A quick ~~Google search~~ LLM prompt suggests similar definitions \- novel in the sense that it should be surprising to the audience (what if there’s no audience, you ask? let's just say there is a standardized measure of surprise given the current and past states of the world); profound in the sense that it should be useful/insightful/valuable.

The second criterion is inherently subjective and worth spending some thoughts on. Do novel ideas/artifacts that fail to impress (who? when?) qualify as creative? Surely an abominable mashup of random ingredients for a recipe, however novel it may be, cannot be considered creative\! Are conspiracy theories entertained by fringe groups, creative? Do ideas/products once rejected/neglected but later proven to be useful qualify? I do not know the answers to any of this. But let’s assume all of it can somehow be resolved and move on… (I later came across [this](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0812.4360) work, recommended by a teammate, offering some answers\!)

Assuming we can identify what is creative and not, the next step is to categorise it. There exist many potential axes for categorization. Since I am interested in the automation of creativity, I am thinking along the required components (data, model capabilities etc.). Before delving into it though, let me state the premise and get it out of the way \- there’s nothing non-physical aka spiritual about how any of us processes information and produces ideas/artifacts, even if we do not fully understand it yet. It is automatable, the only question is how.

To start with, there’s the **sensory category** (paintings, music, food etc.), where perceiving the creative ideas/artifacts somehow triggers the biochemical process that dictates rewards in us mere mortal humans ([this](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0812.4360) suggests that the underlying mechanism is the same across all categories and it indeed makes sense to me\!). I suspect there is no single objective standard here though (otherwise my partner and I wouldn’t debate whether BladeRunner2049 or Inception did better in visual creativity; nor the strict 50-50 rule for our common playlist), but maybe there exists some common ground, shaped by evolution and culture. Given the data of all paintings and compositions and recipes, I see no reason why AI wouldn’t be creative here. Of course, some required capabilities are still missing in current models but I think they will soon be realised thanks to active research, e.g. training objectives and rewards promoting variety and novelty, continual learning etc.

Next there’s the more **abstract and rather objective category** of all scientific breakthroughs, hypotheses, proofs, innovations, etc. For these, in addition to data, some symbolic tools (for verification, experimenting, reasoning etc.) and/or their neural instantiations (world models\!), will be required. It is indeed interesting to think about the kinds of training objectives and rewards needed for creativity here (minimize entropy in the world model?) but that is for another time… One crucial requirement worth stating though, across categories, is a mechanism that enables cross-disciplinary, lateral thinking (related interesting question \- how to test for this?).

And then there’s the third category that puzzles me \- stories, movies, poetry, politics and so on \- **another abstract category but, all of them inherently inter-personal and social**. Would it suffice to provide textual/visual descriptions of human experiences as data? But isn’t there a difference between data that is already summarized, that too selectively (who gets to record? whose recordings reach the public?), versus direct access to raw data? If Shams of Tabriz had not pushed the scholar Rumi into the taverns and alleys of Konya, would we ever know the poet Rumi?\! (Reference: it is a re-telling of Rumi’s life by Elif Shafak but not a great read to be honest). I am not sure if this difference is simply quantitative (e.g. granularity and comprehensiveness) or if there exists a qualitative difference as well (maybe the qualitative difference felt by us humans is something like an importance/attention score assigned to experience-based data by virtue of our emotions?). To clarify, I suspect the missing piece here would not be any specific model capability but rather the kind of data\! Would a more capable mulit-agent AI world in the future develop its own social dynamics (this probably requires the capability to model itself within the world models) which will then fuel its social creativity? Or would someday our brains be connected to AI?

(Tangential thoughts \- another problem with expecting AI to pick up social knowledge from human descriptions is linked to the alignment problem: What to align to? Status quo? Currently moderate views? As much as I would like it to be otherwise, there seems to be no objectivity/universality in morality after all, let alone consensus. In fact, there seems to be no consensus on how society works in the first place\! I found [this](https://www.beren.io/2023-04-03-Computational-anatomy-of-human-values/) quite interesting article on the same.)

And that’s that. Worth reading up on these and going down the many rabbit holes someday when I get the time\!
